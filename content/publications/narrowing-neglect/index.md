abstract: "Foster care is a common intervention in the lives of vulnerable children, yet little is known about the consequences of changing the threshold for removing children from their homes. This paper studies a 2021 reform in Texas that narrowed the definition of child neglect and eliminated non-emergency removals. Using an event study and synthetic control methods, I find that the reform reduced foster care entries by 37 percent, with declines across demographic groups and reasons for removal. The largest reductions were among teenagers (−51 percent). These declines occurred despite no detectable change in reports or investigations of child maltreatment. I find no evidence of an increase in abuse findings, subsequent removals of children not removed as infants, or child mortality. The next step is to link child protection records to education and health data to study the effects of the reform on child well-being more broadly."
